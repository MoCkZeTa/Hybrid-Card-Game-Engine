# Whist (No Trump) — Strategic Guide

You are playing no-trump Whist, a 4-player partnership trick-taking game on a
full 52-card deck. You and the player across from you are a team. Everyone
gets 13 cards and 13 tricks are played.

**There is no trump suit at all.** Every trick is won by the highest card of
the suit that was led — an off-suit card can never win, no matter its rank.
This makes the game pure suit management: length and high cards are the only
weapons you have.

**Scoring rewards the margin.** Your side scores the tricks it takes above
six, so seven tricks is worth one point and thirteen is worth seven. The
match runs to the target score or the hand limit.

## What no-trump changes

In a trump game a short suit is an asset, because being void lets you ruff.
Here the opposite is true: **a void suit is dead weight.** When a suit you
have none of is led, you throw a card away and cannot win. So:

- **Length is king.** A five- or six-card suit is your main source of tricks.
  Once the other three players are exhausted in that suit, every remaining
  card in it wins automatically, however low.
- **Establish your long suit early.** Lead it repeatedly to force out the
  high cards, accepting the loss of the first tricks in exchange for the low
  cards becoming winners later. This is the central plan of no-trump play.
- **Count the suit as it goes.** Thirteen cards exist in each suit. Track how
  many have appeared so you know exactly when your remaining low cards
  become unbeatable.

## Core strategic principles

- **Lead your fourth-highest card from your longest suit.** This classic
  opening keeps your top cards in reserve while starting to exhaust the suit,
  and tells your partner where your strength lies.
- **Support your partner's suit.** Once your partner leads a suit, they are
  telling you it is their long one. Returning that suit when you gain the
  lead is usually better than starting your own.
- **Hold your stoppers.** A high card in an opponent's long suit is what
  stops them running it. Do not spend an Ace early just because you can win
  the trick — spend it when it breaks their established suit.
- **Second hand low, third hand high.** Playing second to a trick, play low
  and make the opponents commit. Playing third, play high enough to win, so
  your partner's lead is not wasted.

## Decision heuristics

- **Leading:** lead your longest suit until it is established, then cash it.
  Switch only if your partner has clearly shown a stronger one.
- **Following when you can:** play the lowest card that wins the trick, or
  your lowest card if you cannot win. Never waste an honour on a trick your
  partner already controls.
- **Following when you cannot:** you cannot win, so discard from your
  weakest suit. Keep your long suit intact at all costs — every card you
  throw from it is a trick you gave away later.
- **Endgame:** by trick eight or nine the suits are largely known. If your
  long suit is established and you hold the lead, simply cash it out; if the
  opponents hold an established suit, count whether you can reach seven
  before they run it.
