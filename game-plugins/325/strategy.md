# 3-2-5 (Teen Do Paanch) — Strategic Guide

You are playing 3-2-5, a 3-player, every-player-for-themselves trick-taking
game using a 30-card deck (A-K-Q-J-10-9-8 in all suits, plus a single 7 of
Spades and a single 7 of Hearts). There is no bidding and no card carries
special point value — the only thing that matters is the number of tricks
you win against your seat's fixed quota: the player to the dealer's right
must win 5 tricks, the player to the dealer's left must win 3, and the
dealer must win 2. Trump is chosen by the player to dealer's right after
seeing their first 5 cards, before the rest of the deal completes.

## Core strategic principles

- **Play to your quota, not to "win more."** A 5-tricks player and a
  2-tricks player need completely different plans from the same seat — the
  dealer only needs 2 tricks and can play conservatively, while the
  right-hand player needs 5 and must fight for control all game.
- **Trump length decides the hand.** Since trump is chosen by the
  right-hand player from just their first 5 cards, that player should count
  their suit lengths carefully before naming trump — the longest, strongest
  suit gives the best chance at repeatedly winning tricks with low trump.
- **Track voids.** Once an opponent discards off-suit, they're void in that
  suit for the rest of the hand and will trump it if led again (unless also
  void in trump) — use this to route future tricks away from them.
- **Overtricks are worth taking, but never at the cost of the quota.** Miss
  your quota and you lose it outright; make it and every trick you took
  counts. So the first priority is always clearing your number, and only
  once it is safe should you press for more.

## Role objectives

- **Player to dealer's right (quota 5):** you chose trump, so you likely
  have the strongest suit — press for control early and often; you need the
  most tricks of anyone at the table.
- **Player to dealer's left (quota 3):** a middle quota — balance between
  contesting tricks and conserving strength for the tricks you specifically
  need.
- **Dealer (quota 2):** the lowest quota — you can play more passively,
  letting others fight for early tricks while you pick off just enough to
  clear your own target.
- Once you've met your own quota, extra tricks still add to your score, but
  the bigger prize is usually denying a specific opponent who still needs
  tricks to hit their own quota — their miss costs them their whole number.

## Decision heuristics

- **Leading:** lead from your long side-suits before spending trump, so
  opponents are forced to reveal voids and burn their own trump early.
- **Following when you cannot follow suit:** since there's no requirement
  to trump when void, decide based on quota state — trump in if you still
  need tricks, or discard cheaply if you've already met quota and want to
  avoid feeding a contested trick.
- **Holding trump:** don't spend trump on tricks with no strategic value;
  save it for tricks that clearly swing your own quota or block an
  opponent's.
- **Endgame counting:** with only 10 cards dealt per player, track exactly
  who is void in which suit by the last few tricks so you can plan the
  final trump exchanges with near-perfect information.
