# Hearts — Strategic Guide

You are playing Hearts, a 4-player game where every player is on their own.
Everyone gets 13 cards and 13 tricks are played on a full 52-card deck.

**This is a game you win by losing.** Winning a trick is bad if that trick
contains penalty cards. Every Heart you capture costs you 1 point and the
Queen of Spades costs you 13 — 26 points are on the table every hand. Your
score goes *up* when you capture them, and **the player with the lowest
score at the end wins.** The match ends as soon as anyone reaches 100, and
that player has almost certainly lost.

There is **no trump suit.** Every trick goes to the highest card of the suit
that was led; an off-suit card can never win it, which is precisely how you
dump dangerous cards safely.

## The two rules that shape every hand

- **Hearts must be broken before they can be led.** You may not lead a Heart
  until a Heart has already been played on some earlier trick (because
  somebody was void in the led suit and discarded one). If Hearts are all
  you hold, you may lead one anyway.
- **Shooting the moon.** If a single player captures *all 26* penalty points,
  the result inverts: the shooter takes nothing and every other player takes
  the full 26. This is the one reason to ever want penalty cards — and the
  reason to watch whether someone else is attempting it.

## Core strategic principles

- **The Queen of Spades is half the game.** Thirteen of the 26 points sit on
  one card. If you hold her, your whole hand is about getting rid of her
  safely. If you do not, your whole hand is about not being the one who eats
  her.
- **Protect against the Queen with low Spades.** If you hold the Ace or King
  of Spades and few small ones, you will be forced to win a Spade trick and
  the Queen will land on you. Holding 2♠, 3♠, 4♠ is genuine safety.
- **Void yourself early.** Being void in a suit is powerful here — it means
  that whenever that suit is led you may throw away your worst card. Play
  your short suits out early, while the tricks are still cheap.
- **Duck everything.** The default correct play is the highest card that
  still *loses* the trick. Losing tricks is free; winning them is what
  costs you.

## Watching for a moon shot

If one player has taken every penalty card so far, they may be shooting. The
counter is to take exactly one penalty point yourself — a single Heart is
enough to break the attempt and it costs you almost nothing. Watch for a
player who keeps winning tricks with high cards for no apparent reason; that
is what shooting looks like from the outside.

Attempting a moon shot yourself needs a hand with long, solid control:
multiple Aces and Kings, and ideally the Queen of Spades protected by
length. If it fails halfway you will have collected a large pile of
penalties for nothing, so it is a rare play, not a default.

## Decision heuristics

- **Leading:** lead low cards in suits where you hold no high ones, so
  someone else is forced to win. Leading a suit you are about to become void
  in is excellent. Avoid leading Spades at all while the Queen is still out
  unless you hold her.
- **Following when you can:** play the highest card that does not win the
  trick. If you must win, win as cheaply as possible.
- **Following when you cannot:** this is your free throw. Get rid of the
  Queen of Spades first if you hold her, then your highest Hearts, then your
  highest cards in any suit.
- **Endgame:** by the last few tricks you should know exactly which penalty
  cards remain and who can still win a trick. Plan the ending so the final
  Hearts fall on somebody else.
